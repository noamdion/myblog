## What Can We Really Know About the World?

I am reading *Range* by David Epstein. I think it is a good book, but I don't agree with everything the author says.

The world is complex. No matter how much we learn, we can never understand everything. Every author chooses certain studies, examples, and ideas to explain the world. Even if a book contains a hundred examples, those examples cannot cover every possible situation.

A book may help us understand part of the world, but that doesn't mean it shows us the whole picture.

## The World Is Like a Broken Mirror

In my opinion, the world is like a broken mirror. Everyone tries to pick up as many pieces as they can.

Each piece reflects a real part of the world. One person may understand human behavior, while another may understand science, history, or everyday life. Their knowledge may be different, but each person can see something valuable.

However, even if we put all the pieces together, we may never rebuild the original mirror perfectly. We might still misunderstand how everything fits together.

Sometimes, one small piece of a mirror may give us a clearer view of one thing than five pieces put together in the wrong way. More information doesn't always mean better understanding.

This is why I believe we need wisdom. We need to know not only what we understand, but also what we don't know.

## Every Star Has Its Own Light

If the world is like a broken mirror, perhaps people are also like stars in the sky.

Every star has its own light. One star may be small, but that doesn't mean its light has no value. When we look at the whole sky, we see many stars shining together.

People are similar. We have different experiences, knowledge, and ways of thinking. We don't need to think in exactly the same way. Instead, we can talk to each other, share our ideas, and learn from one another.

One person cannot understand everything, but many people can help one another see more of the world.

We may never see the complete picture, but together we can understand more than we could alone.

So we should be proud of ourselves. We don't have to be the smartest person in the room or know everything about the world. We can still contribute something valuable.

## Don't Treat Any Book as the Bible

That's why I don't treat any book as the Bible.

A bestseller is not necessarily a completely correct book. An author may be intelligent and well educated, but that doesn't mean every conclusion is right. Even a good author can choose examples that support an argument while missing other important situations.

I respect authors, and I want to learn from them. But I also want to think for myself.

When I read *Range*, I try to understand Epstein's ideas. At the same time, I ask whether those ideas apply to everyone or only to certain people and environments.

I don't need to reject the whole book just because I disagree with some parts. I can learn something valuable from an author without accepting everything he says.

For me, reading is not about finding someone who has all the answers. It is about discovering new ideas and learning how to think more carefully.

## Wisdom Lies in Simplicity

I also believe that truly understanding something means being able to explain it clearly.

Difficult ideas sometimes require difficult words, but complicated language doesn't automatically mean deep thinking. If we understand an idea well, we should try to explain it in simple language without losing its meaning.

Perhaps *Range* is a good book, but it may not be suitable for everyone in its original form. Some readers may find its vocabulary and sentence structures difficult. That doesn't mean they are less intelligent or unable to understand its main ideas.

A good idea should not belong only to highly educated people. More people should have the chance to understand it.

For me, the goal is not to read a book and agree with everything in it. The goal is to understand the author's ideas, examine the evidence, and decide what I think.

## Keep Learning, Keep Thinking

I will keep reading books, even when they are difficult. But I will not treat any author as the final authority on the truth.

I will respect what others know, remain open to new ideas, and remember that my own understanding is also incomplete.

The world may always be more complex than the picture we create in our minds. We may never put every piece of the mirror back in its original place.

But we can keep learning from one another.

We can be humble about what we don't know and proud of what we can contribute.

Perhaps that is enough: not to understand the whole world, but to keep trying to understand it better, one piece at a time.
